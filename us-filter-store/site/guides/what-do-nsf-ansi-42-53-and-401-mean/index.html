What do NSF/ANSI 42, 53 and 401 mean?

Filter packaging often lists NSF/ANSI numbers. They are third-party standards that describe what a filter has been tested to reduce. Here is what the common ones mean.

NSF/ANSI 42

Covers aesthetic effects: chlorine taste and odor and particulates.

NSF/ANSI 53

Covers health effects: certain contaminants such as lead, cysts, and others that depend on the product.

NSF/ANSI 401

Covers some emerging contaminants, such as certain pharmaceuticals and other compounds.

NSF/ANSI P473 and others

Some standards cover specific substances such as PFOA and PFOS. Which standards a particular filter meets is stated by the filter's manufacturer on the packaging.

How to use this

Look at the claims and certifications printed on the specific filter you are buying. Do not assume two filters that look alike have the same certifications.

Frequently asked questions

Does every refrigerator filter have an NSF certification?

No. Certifications are product-specific. Check the packaging of the filter you are considering.

Is an NSF mark a guarantee for all contaminants?

No. A certification applies only to the substances and conditions listed in the standard and the product's performance data sheet.
