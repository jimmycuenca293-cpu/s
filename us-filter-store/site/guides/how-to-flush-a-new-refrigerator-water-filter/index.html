How to flush a new refrigerator water filter

A new carbon filter contains fine carbon dust and trapped air. Flushing clears both so the first glass of water is clear and tastes right.

What to do

Place a large pitcher or container under the dispenser and run water until the flow is steady. Most manufacturers ask for a few gallons; your manual gives the exact amount for your model.

Do this in short bursts if the dispenser has an automatic timeout: pressing the lever, releasing it and repeating is normal.

What you may see

  • Sputtering or spitting in the first few seconds is trapped air.
  • Gray or cloudy water at first can be fine carbon particles. It should clear after flushing.
  • If the water stays cloudy or particles keep coming, stop and check that the filter is seated correctly.

Do not forget the ice

Ice made before the filter change can hold old taste. Discard the first batches of ice after changing the filter.

Frequently asked questions

How many gallons should I flush?

It depends on the brand and model. Many guides ask for about 2 to 4 gallons of water, but check your owner's manual.

Is the first water safe to drink?

Follow the filter and refrigerator manufacturers' instructions. Flushing is meant to be done before regular use.

Do I need to flush the ice maker too?

Discard the first few batches of ice after a filter change so any residual taste or carbon dust clears out.
