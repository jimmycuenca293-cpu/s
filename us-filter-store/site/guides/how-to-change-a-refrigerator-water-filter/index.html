How to change a refrigerator water filter (step by step)

Replacing a refrigerator water filter takes about five minutes and needs no tools. The exact motion depends on the model, but the steps below apply to most internal filters.

Before you start

Have the new filter and a towel ready. Take the protective cap off the new filter only when you are about to install it.

Check your owner's manual for the filter location. It is usually inside the fresh-food compartment (upper corner or back wall) or in the base grille at the bottom front.

Steps

Locate the old filter and, if there is a cover, open it.

Turn the filter counterclockwise (or push and release the button, depending on the model) and pull it straight out. A little water may drip: have the towel ready.

Remove the cap from the new filter and check that the O-rings are in place.

Align the new filter with the opening and push it in, then turn it clockwise until it stops or clicks into place.

Close the cover and reset the filter indicator light (see your brand's reset guide).

Flush the new filter by running water through the dispenser for the amount recommended in your manual, usually a few gallons.

Quick steps

  1. Locate the old filter and open the cover if there is one.
  2. Turn the filter counterclockwise or press the release button and pull it straight out.
  3. Remove the protective cap from the new filter and check the O-rings.
  4. Align the new filter, push it in and turn it clockwise until it locks.
  5. Close the cover and reset the filter indicator light.
  6. Flush the filter by running water through the dispenser as recommended in the manual.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to turn off the water to change the filter?

Most internal refrigerator filters do not require shutting off the water. Follow your manual; some models ask you to turn the water supply valve off first.

Why does the water sputter after the change?

Air trapped in the line. Keep running water through the dispenser until the stream is steady and clear.

Can I change the filter myself?

Yes. It is designed to be a homeowner task and needs no tools.
